SYMPTOM

Trouble sleeping

Why it happens

Falling estrogen and progesterone affect sleep directly, and night sweats wake you up. Stress and mood changes at this stage make it harder to fall back asleep.

What helps

Day to day

  • Go to bed and wake up at the same time, even on weekends.
  • Keep the room cool and dark, and put your phone away an hour before bed.
  • Avoid caffeine after noon and alcohol close to bedtime.

Natural options

  • Magnesium helps some women relax and sleep better. See the Supplements page for which form to look for.

Treatments to ask about

  • If night sweats are what wake you, treating them often fixes the sleep too.
  • Ask about therapy for insomnia (CBT-I). It works well and has no side effects.

See a doctor if…

  • You’ve slept badly most nights for more than a few weeks.
  • You snore loudly or stop breathing in your sleep. That can be sleep apnea, which becomes more common after menopause.
